Section 02

Executive summary

Energy Flow gives filers one place to register, file, pay, track and respond, and gives staff one inbox and workflow to check, route, review, decide, docket and publish every submission.

  • Two ways in. People file through guided web screens. Companies send structured data (eTariff, EQR, MBR in XML; Forms 1, 2, 6, 60 and 714 in XBRL) system to system through a web API.
  • One lifecycle. Every submission gets an accession number, follows one status model and keeps a full activity history.
  • Three processing paths. Web filings pass an Office of the Secretary (OSEC) check, then a program office review and a recorded decision. eTariff goes straight to a program office. EQR, MBR and the five XBRL forms are accepted automatically once system checks pass, and the owning office is notified.
  • Routing by type. Electric to OEMR, Hydro to OEP, Pipeline to OER, all other web filings to OGC; any of OEMR, OEP, OER, OE or OGC can take a rehearing.
  • Program office review. Each office acknowledges receipt, recommends accept or reject, gives docketing and eLibrary instructions, and can send the filer a data request.
  • Publication. Accepted content is published to eLibrary, a separate document system. For EQR, MBR and the XBRL forms only a cover letter goes to eLibrary; the data goes to its own database.
  • Organizations and roles. One account can act for several companies as Organization Administrator, Filing Representative or Preparer.
  • Filing calendar. Filers see what they must file and when, why each item applies, how ready they are, and what they already submitted; staff see submissions, response deadlines, stage targets and acceptances by date. Both choose a Month, Quarter or Year view.
  • Help assistant. An in-page assistant answers questions and can open pages, find filings and start tasks, but never submits or decides anything.
  • Monitoring. Staff see an end-to-end pipeline, bottlenecks by office and stage, workload by analyst, and outcome dashboards.
Section 03

Scope, assumptions and constraints

The scope is the customer-facing portal plus the internal intake and review workflow that decides acceptance; regulatory systems of record stay outside it.

In scope

  • Registration, sign-in with a second factor, profile, organizations and roles
  • Guided web filing: type selection, payment hand-off, upload, parties, contacts, description, validation, certification, submission
  • Filing history, status tracking, responses to requests, supplements, withdrawal, rehearing requests, notices
  • Web API intake for eTariff, EQR, MBR and Forms 1, 2, 6, 60 and 714
  • Staff inbox, routing, program office workflow, decision, docketing hand-off, eLibrary hand-off, acceptance notifications
  • Dashboards, audit history and the help assistant
  • The filing calendar: My Filing Calendar for filers and the staff calendar, each with Month, Quarter and Year views

Out of scope (integrated, not rebuilt)

  • eLibrary document management, the docket system of record, Pay.gov, the EQR, MBR and XBRL form databases, identity provider, email delivery, Commission agenda management
  • Appeals and judicial review, which happen outside the portal
  • Comment and Subscription modules beyond notification hooks (named in the deck, not built in the prototype)
  • Reading orders and notices automatically to propose calendar deadlines (a later phase in the calendar deck)
  • Two-way synchronization with Outlook or other calendar products; the portal offers export only

Assumptions

  • The prototype's stages, targets, work-in-progress limits, validation rules, docket number format and accession number format are placeholders, not agency rules.
  • The calendar's due-date schedules, applicability rules, regulatory basis text, sample dockets and the sample order are placeholders. The calendar informs filers; the governing regulation or order remains the authority on any deadline.
  • Office names follow the client's usage. ferc.gov lists OER as the Office of Electric Reliability and the enforcement office as OERA; this needs confirming.
  • The portal is the system of engagement; the record of each docket, document and payment stays in its own system.

Constraints

  • Federal website standards (USWDS banner and identifier, required links), Section 508 accessibility, FISMA and NIST SP 800-53 security controls, NARA records schedules.
  • No card or bank details are collected by the portal; payment happens on Pay.gov.

Section 06

Business process and submission lifecycle

Every submission is received and numbered the same way, then takes one of three paths: OSEC check and program office review for web filings, straight to a program office for eTariff, or automatic acceptance for EQR, MBR and the XBRL forms.

Only the decision step records acceptance for web filings and eTariff; for EQR, MBR and the forms, passing the system checks is the acceptance.

Exception paths

  • Information request (OSEC): the filer corrects and resubmits; the filing returns to Received.
  • Data request (program office): the filing waits in its stage; the filer's response returns it to the same office and marks it Amended.
  • Reroute: an office can send a filing to another office's Intake; review steps start over there.
  • Hold: an accepted filing whose instruction is to hold waits in Held from eLibrary until OSEC releases it.
  • Withdrawal: the filer can withdraw until the filing is docketed.
  • Follow-on filings: supplements and rehearing requests start new filings linked to the original.

Section 10

Logical architecture

The proposed target is a set of application services over one operational database, behind an identity provider and API gateway, handing records to the systems of record through an outbound queue. The prototype implements the service logic in the browser only.

Design principles

  • The portal is the system of engagement; dockets, documents, payments and structured data stay in their systems of record.
  • Every state change goes through one service and writes one event, so history, notices and dashboards agree.
  • Hand-offs are asynchronous and retried; a failed hand-off is visible to staff, never silent.
  • Rules (routing, stages, targets, validation, API types, due-date schedules, applicability) live in reference data, not code.
  • Calendar items are computed from rules, organization data, dockets and submissions each time they are shown; only the organization's decisions (applicability, assignments, milestones, reminders) are stored.

Proposed API intake contract (INT-03)

  1. The company system signs in with its organization credential and sends one submission per request: type, organization, reporting period where it applies, and the XML or XBRL payload.
  2. The portal issues an accession number and runs the checks in BR-12, BR-13 and BR-16.
  3. The response is synchronous: accepted (with accession), routed to a program office (eTariff), or rejected (with the failed rules).
  4. The submission appears in the organization's filings marked Sent by API.

Endpoint names, schemas and taxonomies for each type need to come from the owners of the existing eTariff, EQR, MBR and XBRL services; this document does not specify them.

Section 14

Help assistant

The help assistant guides and navigates but never acts on a record: it can read the person's situation, open pages and filings, and open a task's start page, and the person always presses the button that changes anything.

How it works

  1. A Help button sits on every page; the panel shows suggested questions for the person's role.
  2. Each question goes to the AI service with standing instructions, a site guide, the person's current situation (role, page, organization, drafts, filings needing action) and the last ten turns.
  3. The AI may call page tools; each tool runs in the browser with the person's own rights and its effect is shown under the reply (for example "Opened S-1008").
  4. If the AI service is unavailable or the person declines it, built-in guided answers with action buttons take over.

Tools offered to the AI

Tool Who gets it What it does Limits
get_situation Everyone Returns role, page, organization context, draft progress, filings needing action Own data only
go_to_page Everyone Opens a page Only pages allowed for the role
find_filings Signed-in users Searches filings by number, docket, accession or words; returns up to 8 Filers: filings they may see
open_filing Signed-in users Opens one filing on the right page Filers: filings they may see
open_task_start Filers Opens the start page with new, respond, supplement or rehearing selected Creates and submits nothing

Safeguards

  • No tool can submit, pay, certify, accept, reject, withdraw, publish or edit.
  • Filing descriptions and other site data are passed as data, and the instructions say so.
  • Replies are shown as plain text, never as markup.
  • Each answer is a new call with no memory beyond the turns the page sends.
  • In production the AI endpoint, data handling and retention must be approved by the agency; the prototype uses Claude through the viewer's own Claude account.
